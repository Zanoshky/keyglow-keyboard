Sources and licences

Everything the Keyglow app ships that it did not make itself, with its licence. The app's credits, under Settings, Help, link here.

word lists

The 50,000 most used words in 23 languages, with how often each is used.

  • From: wordfreq by Robyn Speer, with data from Wikipedia, OpenSubtitles, news, books and web text.
  • Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Keyglow's word lists are shared under the same licence, CC BY-SA 4.0.

more words

Up to 150,000 words in all for languages with many word forms (Croatian, Slovak, Slovenian, Czech, Polish, Finnish, Hungarian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Turkish, Romanian) and 100,000 for the rest, added after the wordfreq words and counted from Wikipedia text. German words written with ss get their ß back where Wikipedia writes them so.

  • From: Wikipedia, by its contributors, the same article text as the word pairs below.
  • Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0.

word pairs

Which word usually follows which in 23 languages, and which words usually start a sentence, as a table of chances. Counted from about 20 to 115 million words of Wikipedia articles per language, mixed with the everyday sentences of Tatoeba, which carry the chat Wikipedia lacks.

  • From: Wikipedia, by its contributors, as the cleaned article text of the 1 November 2023 dumps in the wikimedia/wikipedia set.
  • Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0. The Wikimedia dump terms license the text under the GFDL and CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Also from: Tatoeba, by its contributors, the per language sentence exports. Croatian also reads the Bosnian and Latin script Serbian ones.
  • Tatoeba licence: CC BY 2.0 FR (some sentences are CC0), as the Tatoeba terms of use say.
  • Keyglow's word pair tables are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.

words never suggested

Swear and offensive words: you can still type them and Keyglow keeps them, but never offers them.

  • From: the List of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene and Otherwise Bad Words (LDNOOBW).
  • Licence: CC BY 4.0.
